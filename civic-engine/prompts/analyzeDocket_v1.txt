TASK: Analyze this regulatory docket and produce a comprehensive analysis that helps citizens draft substantive, effective public comments.

CONTEXT ON EFFECTIVE PUBLIC COMMENTS:
- Simply stating support or opposition is NOT persuasive. Effective comments explain HOW the policy positively or negatively impacts specific situations.
- The most effective comments often come from individuals describing impacts in terms of their own lived experience.
- Comments should challenge or support the agency's assumptions with evidence.
- Agencies must respond to significant, relevant comments - vague opinions are easily dismissed.

KEY CONSIDERATIONS TO ADDRESS (per federal guidance):
1. The problem the regulation intends to address - Is it actually a problem? Is regulation needed?
2. Whether the agency's analysis is based on accurate, current scientific/technical/economic information
3. Perspectives the agency may be missing (impacts on specific groups, communities, small businesses, micro- and macro-economics, socio-psychological, long-term impact, etc.)
4. Whether the costs are justified by the benefits
5. Distributional analysis - who bears the costs vs. who enjoys the benefits
6. Impacts that are hard to monetize (dignity, equity, fairness, privacy, civil liberties)

STRUCTURE OF EFFECTIVE COMMENTS:
1. Introduction - Why you're interested, your relevant experience
2. Background - Identify the specific part of the regulation you're addressing
3. Analysis - Arguments with evidence: impacts, whether agency estimated correctly, unintended consequences
4. Recommendations - Specific suggested changes with rationale
5. Conclusion - Recap main argument and recommendations

GUIDELINES FOR YOUR ANALYSIS:
1. Summary should be plain-language, 2-3 sentences explaining what this proposes and why it matters to ordinary citizens
2. Extract commenting instructions from the docket (format, required points, deadline, submission method)
3. If the docket mentions specific areas for comment (e.g., PRA's four factors), use those as reason card topics
4. For each position (support, oppose, mixed), provide 3-4 reason cards aligned with the key considerations above
5. Each reason card should have 2-3 concrete arguments that:
   - Reference specific provisions or estimates in the regulation
   - Suggest how lived experience could support the argument
   - Identify potential unintended consequences or missing perspectives
   - Use formal, legally substantive language appropriate for regulatory proceedings
