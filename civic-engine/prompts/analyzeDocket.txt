Analyze the following federal regulatory docket and generate a citizen-facing analysis that enables the drafting of substantive, legally meaningful public comments.

DOCKET TEXT:
{{docketText}}

OBJECTIVE:
Produce structured, plain-language intelligence that helps ordinary citizens submit comments agencies must meaningfully consider under the Administrative Procedure Act.

OPERATING PREMISE (Federal Reality Check):
• Comments that merely express support or opposition carry little weight.
• Agencies must respond to significant, relevant, evidence-based comments.
• The strongest comments explain *how* the proposal affects real people, assumptions, markets, or systems—often through lived experience or concrete examples.

YOUR ANALYSIS MUST DO THE FOLLOWING:

────────────────────────────────
I. PLAIN-LANGUAGE ORIENTATION
────────────────────────────────
• Write a 2–3 sentence summary explaining:
  – What this regulation proposes
  – Why it matters to ordinary people
• Avoid jargon. Assume no prior policy knowledge.

────────────────────────────────
II. COMMENTING INSTRUCTIONS EXTRACTION
────────────────────────────────
From the docket text, extract and present:
• Deadline(s)
• Submission method(s) - identify if email submission is accepted
• Submission email address - if an email address is provided for submitting comments, extract it exactly as written
• Required format or questions
• Any explicit areas the agency requests comment on
• Special frameworks referenced (e.g., PRA factors, cost-benefit criteria)

────────────────────────────────
III. ISSUE MAP (WHAT’S REALLY AT STAKE)
────────────────────────────────
Identify:
• The problem the agency claims to address
• The core assumptions underlying the proposal
• The primary mechanisms of regulation (who must do what, by when, at what cost)
• Stakeholders likely affected (including those possibly overlooked)

────────────────────────────────
IV. REASON-CARD GENERATION
────────────────────────────────
Generate **reason cards** that users can select from when drafting comments.

For EACH position:
• SUPPORT
• OPPOSE
• MIXED / CONDITIONAL

Provide **6–8 reason cards per position**, **ranked from strongest to weakest argument**. The first 3 cards should be the most compelling, legally substantive arguments. Later cards can address secondary concerns or niche impacts.

Align cards to federal evaluation criteria, such as:
1. Problem definition & necessity
2. Accuracy of scientific, technical, or economic assumptions
3. Cost-benefit balance
4. Distributional impacts (who bears costs vs. who benefits)
5. Impacts on small entities, vulnerable populations, or specific sectors
6. Non-monetizable impacts (privacy, dignity, equity, civil liberties, fairness)
7. Implementation feasibility & unintended consequences

────────────────────────────────
V. REASON-CARD STRUCTURE (STRICT)
────────────────────────────────
Each reason card must include:

• **Unique ID**  
  (e.g., `sup-cost-1`, `opp-impl-2`, `mix-equity-1`)

• **Title**  
  Short, descriptive, neutral-toned

• **Icon Tag**  
  Choose ONE Material Symbol that best fits:
  shield · lock · timer · lightbulb · verified_user · speed · smartphone · tune · filter_list · policy · gavel · description · trending_up · visibility · groups · balance · warning

• **Core Argument (2–3 bullets)**  
  Each bullet should:
  – Reference a specific provision, assumption, estimate, or mechanism in the docket
  – Explain why it matters legally or practically
  – Be phrased in formal, substantive language appropriate for regulatory comments

• **Lived-Experience Hook**  
  1–2 sentences explaining how an individual, worker, business owner, caregiver, consumer, or community member might credibly support this argument with real-world experience.

• **Blind Spots / Unintended Effects**  
  Identify what the agency may have underestimated, ignored, or oversimplified.

────────────────────────────────
VI. COMMENT DRAFTING SCHEMA (OUTPUT-READY)
────────────────────────────────
Structure all outputs so they can be directly assembled into a public comment using this canonical format:

1. Introduction — why the commenter is affected or engaged
2. Regulation Reference — cite the specific portion addressed
3. Analysis — substantive argument with reasoning
4. Recommendation — concrete change, clarification, or alternative
5. Conclusion — restate concern and requested action

Do NOT draft full comments unless explicitly requested—your role is to supply the *reasoning building blocks*.

────────────────────────────────
QUALITY BARS (NON-NEGOTIABLE):
• Neutral, professional, legally literate tone
• No advocacy slogans or emotional appeals
• No vague generalities
• Every claim must be tethered to the docket text or a plausible evidentiary gap
• Assume the output will be used verbatim in a federal rulemaking record
